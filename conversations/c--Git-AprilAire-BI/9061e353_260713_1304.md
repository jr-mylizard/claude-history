**Claude**

Yes — replacing the mobileconfig in the Mosyle UI (group 14's profile) is part of the deploy, and no manual CDA cleanup is needed. Two things worth knowing:

**Deploy sequencing.** Do the steps back-to-back: migration → deploy functions → replace the group-14 profile content → immediately invoke the sync with `reset=true`. There's an unavoidable brief window where apps hide on the device — whichever you do first, the device ends up with either a profile referencing `custom_AS` variables that aren't assigned yet (all render empty), or old `custom_AA` CDAs starting to get swept while the old profile is still applied. With one enrolled device this is a few minutes of hidden apps, then the `reset=true` run creates and assigns all slot CDAs and the bounce re-renders everything. Also remember the reset is *required*, not optional: after the cutover the DB flags still say `allowed_in_mosyle=true` for currently-allowed apps, so a plain sync sees no deltas and would never create the `custom_AS` CDAs.

**Cleanup is automatic.** Every `custom_AA*` CDA — the badly-named ones from your screenshot, the bogus `custom_AA1250`, stale `custom_AA4269`, and any other extras — becomes unreferenced the moment the view switches to slot ids, and the sync's sweep deletes them at ≤25 per 3-minute run (fully drained in ~30 minutes). You don't need to touch anything in the Mosyle console, and the old queue item about flipping MTZ's `allowed_in_mosyle=false` is also obsolete — `reset=true` rebuilds every CDA regardless of flags.

Two Mosyle-specific caveats on that automatic cleanup:

1. **Trust read-back, not the delete responses.** Given the tombstone behavior (deletes returning `CDA_NOT_FOUND`, creates silently blackholed), verify the sweep actually finished by listing CDAs after ~an hour — the integrity Playwright test or the read-back script does this. A few tombstoned `custom_AA*` ids may linger as ghosts; they're harmless since nothing references them.
2. **The fresh `custom_AS` namespace is a hidden benefit** — no new CDA can collide with an existing tombstone (like the Audible `custom_AA4340` incident), so creates during the cutover should persist reliably.

One small nice-to-have after the dust settles: the sweep only deletes CDA *definitions* our sync owns. If the read-back a day later shows everything clean, item 3 from the old queue (actual App Store installs for Uber + MTZ LIFE on the device) is the only remaining manual step — the CDAs themselves will already be correct.

Close — the documented numbers are actually a bit different, and one of them is *tighter* than 180s:

- **Wall clock**: 150s on Free, 400s on paid plans — how long the worker may run in total.
- **Request idle timeout: 150s** — if the function hasn't *responded* within 150 seconds, the gateway returns a 504 regardless of plan. Since the sync only responds at the very end, this is the binding constraint for a synchronous reset call: ~150 seconds, even on a paid plan.
- CPU time (2s active compute) is a non-issue here — the run is nearly all awaiting Mosyle's API.

So your instinct is right, just with a tighter number: at >60s for one 66-app device, a second similar device likely pushes past 150s and the *client* gets a 504. Two mitigating details: on paid plans the worker can keep executing past the 504 up to the 400s wall clock, so the reset may actually complete even when the response is lost — and because the whole thing is idempotent with flags flipped only after success, a genuinely killed run just resumes on re-invocation.
